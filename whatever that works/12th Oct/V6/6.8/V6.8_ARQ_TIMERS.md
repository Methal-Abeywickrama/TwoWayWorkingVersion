# v6.8: faster recovery from a lost packet

v6.8 fixes how the ARQ and the CSMA work together. Use `v6.8_cdp_link.grc` with the updated `cdp_link_app.py`. Everything else in `V6.7_GUIDE.md` still applies.

## Why one lost packet stalled the link

The CSMA's own timers are not the problem. The echo timeout (0.1 s) and the unmute timeout (0.1 s) only manage a single burst. The CSMA's "fail → double the backoff" input isn't connected to the ARQ, and should stay unconnected: most losses here come from a weak signal, not collisions, and doubling the backoff would add up to 128 × 15 ms ≈ 1.9 s to every frame.

The problem was that the ARQ couldn't see the CSMA:

1. **The ARQ's timeout clock started too early.** It started when a segment was handed to the CSMA queue, not when it went on air. With 8 segments queued, plus ACKs from the other node competing for the channel, a segment can wait about 1 s before it is sent. The ARQ counted that wait as round-trip time, so its timeout had to stay long: 2 s at first, at least 1 s, and doubling up to 8 s after repeated losses. Shorter timeouts fired while frames were still waiting in the queue.
2. **A timeout resent every overdue segment**, and the resends went to the back of the CSMA queue. A single lost ACK therefore cost a whole window of duplicates plus the wait behind them.
3. **A lost segment with nothing confirmed after it** (the last segment of a message, or a single-segment chat message) could only be recovered by a timeout.
4. **A resent segment that arrived as a duplicate** had its ACK held back by up to 0.5 s, even though the resend asked for an immediate ACK.

## What v6.8 changes

| Where | Change |
|---|---|
| CSMA → ARQ (new connection `tx_start → mac_in`) | When the CSMA releases a frame to the air, it reports the frame's tag. The ARQ starts that segment's timer at that moment and measures round-trip time from it, so queue and backoff time no longer count. |
| CSMA queue | ARQ ACKs and resends carry a `prio` flag and are queued ahead of normal frames. |
| ARQ loss detection | Frames on one shared channel arrive in the order they were sent. So if a segment that went on air *later* is confirmed, every unconfirmed segment sent *earlier* was lost, and is resent at once. This covers several gaps at once, a lost resend, and the end of a message after a timeout. |
| ARQ timeout | Only the oldest overdue segment is resent, with ACK-now. Its ACK carries the receiver's full state, so a lost ACK now costs one duplicate frame. The other overdue segments wait one more timeout for that ACK. |
| Receiver | A segment with ACK-now is acknowledged immediately, even when it is a duplicate. |
| Timer defaults | First timeout **1.0 s** (was 2), shortest **0.5 s** (was 1), longest **4 s** (was 8). These are only safe with on-air timing. |
| Framer | Passes the ARQ's tag and `prio` flag on to the CSMA. |

**If the flowgraph doesn't report on-air times** (a v6.7 flowgraph, or the connection removed), the ARQ falls back to v6.7 behaviour. In that case keep the old timeouts (2 / 1 / 8 s), because short ones cause needless resends.

**Settings saved by the v6.7 app are updated on first start.** The app switches to the v6.8 .grc if it's in the same folder. Timeouts still at the old defaults change to the new ones; values you changed yourself are kept.

## Simulation results (same channel model, CSMA queue per node, 40–110 ms access + airtime)

| Test | v6.7 | v6.8 |
|---|---|---|
| 20 chat messages with 1 lost data frame, 1 lost ACK, and 1 data frame lost twice | worst message 3.2 s, all 20 took 8.6 s | worst 1.8 s, all 20 took 6.4 s |
| 60 kB one way, 15 % loss (3 runs) | 8.9 – 20.0 s, 1–5 timeouts | 9.8 – 10.4 s, 1–2 timeouts |
| 40 kB each way at the same time, 5 % loss (2 runs) | 14.7 – 14.9 s, 10–16 resends | 12.3 – 13.6 s, 1–3 resends |
| Same two-way test, v6.7 with the short timers | 16.4 – 22.6 s, 8–19 needless resends | — |

The last row shows why v6.7 couldn't simply use shorter timeouts.

## Check that it's working

In **Dashboard → Live monitor**, under the ARQ table:

- "**on-air timers on (CSMA reports frames on air)**" should be shown after the first message.
- The "in flight / waiting" column shows "(n still in CSMA queue)" while frames wait to be sent. Their timers don't run during that wait.
- After a loss, `resent` should go up by about one per lost frame, and `timeouts` much more slowly than before.

## If it still stalls

The ARQ can now recover a lost frame in about 0.5–1 s. A stall that lasts much longer comes from something else, and the watchdog names it:

| Watchdog event | Likely cause |
|---|---|
| `csma_queue_waiting` | The CSMA thinks the channel is busy: a noise-floor jump or interference |
| `deframer_waiting` | A received frame held in the receive chain |
| `samples_dropped` | The PC is losing radio samples |
| `rx_stream_stalled` | The radio link dropped |

For any of these, send me the report `.md` and `stacks_node<ID>.txt` from both nodes.
